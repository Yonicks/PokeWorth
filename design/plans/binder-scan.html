"Sure" uses the same rule as a single scan: the best match scores at least 85% and leads the next one by 15 points. That means the name, number, set size and attacks mostly agree. Anything less is never added without your tap.

Under the hood

How detection works

Most of this already exists from the single-card scan. The new parts are cutting the page into pockets, running many pockets at once, and the review grid. They're outlined in gold.

PhotoCamera or imported image, kept at full size (not the 1200 px the single scan uses)
Cut into pocketsThe chosen grid, with a small inset so pocket edges and sleeve seams are left out. Uses the Expo Go image tool's crop.
Empty checkA pocket with almost no text is marked empty and not matched
Read textOn-device OCR in a development build; the free cloud OCR in Expo Go and on web

Two ways to read the page

The free cloud OCR allows 500 requests a day per address and 1 MB per image. It can also return where each word sits on the image. That gives two options, and real binder photos should decide between them.

OptionRequests per 9-pocket pageStrengthWeakness
A · One request per pocket9 (about 55 pages a day in Expo Go)Each pocket is sent at good resolution, so the small 4/102 number stays readableSlower, and uses the daily limit faster. A development build reads on the phone with no limit.
B · One request per page, with word positions1Fast and cheap: words are sorted into pockets by where they sitThe whole page must fit in 1 MB, so the collector numbers may be too small to read
Hybrid · B, then A where needed1 + pockets missing a numberCheap when the photo is sharp, accurate when it isn'tMore code. Only worth it if A or B alone falls short.

Matching also calls the card catalog for each pocket: one name search, plus up to 8 card lookups to compare attacks. TCGdex is free and the results are cached, but a full page makes a few dozen requests. Step 0 measures how long that really takes.

Decision 2 outcome: option A. It does clearly better on full-art cards and reads more collector numbers. The safety rule held: no pocket was marked Sure with the wrong card. The 4% inset and pocket shape worked unchanged, even on the angled page with a cut-off column.

Neither option reaches 7 of 9 on every page yet. Most misses come from the matcher, not from unreadable text, and these fixes go into step 2:

  • Search by number when the name is wrong. Misty's Lapras: the number 050/182 was read, but the attack "Surf" was taken as the name. Look the number up in sets of that size.
  • Trainer names and OCR noise in front of the name. "My Gyarados" for Misty's Gyarados, "STAGEZ Blaziken" and "N. Reshiram": also try the last word ("Gyarados", "Blaziken").
  • Garbled ex suffixes. "Mega Mawile ev", "Pikachuex": treat ev / eX / a glued "ex" as ex.
  • Cut-off names. "Butteri" for Butterfree: try a short prefix search when nothing else matches.
  • Attack names taken as card names. Froakie "Collect", Blaziken "Heat Blast": prefer the line nearest the top of the pocket.
  • The sideways card (Darkrai & Cresselia LEGEND): ask the OCR to detect rotation.

Build

Build steps

Each step ends with tests or something you can try. Step 0 needs your real binder photos.

  1. Try it on real pages done

    You send 3 photos of real binder pages: one easy, one with glare, one at a slight angle. I run options A and B on them outside the app and record, pocket by pocket, whether the right card came out on top, plus the time and request count.

    This decides the OCR option and the pocket inset before any screens are built.

  2. Pocket grid and cropping built

    A pure layout model for 2×2, 3×3 and 3×4 that turns a photo size into pocket rectangles with an inset. A crop service on top of it. Offline tests for the maths.

  3. Batch reading built

    A queue that reads and matches pockets three at a time, with progress, cancel, retry for failed pockets and the empty check. Each pocket gets Sure, Check, Not found or Empty. Also the matcher fixes from step 0: number lookup, last-word and prefix fallbacks, ex suffixes, top-line preference and rotation. Tested with recorded OCR text from your three pages.

  4. Binder camera

    A Card / Binder page switch on the Scan screen, the layout picker, the gold grid guide, the glare tip and photo import. Also reachable from the Portfolio tab.

  5. Review grid

    Pockets laid out like your page, each with a status colour. Tapping a pocket shows the top 3 matches, search by hand, the printing choice, or skip. Sure pockets start ticked; Check pockets you tick yourself.

  6. Add to portfolio

    A tested "add many" in the collection model: duplicates on a page are merged, cards you already own have their quantity raised, and it ends with a summary ("Added 7 cards, $412.30") and Scan next page.

Decisions

Decisions

You accepted all four recommendations on 7 Nov 2026. Step 0 starts when your three binder photos arrive.

1 · Pocket layouts

Most binders use 9-pocket pages; 4-pocket pages are common for big cards, and 12-pocket for mini binders.

Recommendation: 3×3 and 2×2 first; add 3×4 if you use it.

2 · Reading option

A (per pocket), B (whole page) or the hybrid. The trade-off is between the daily limit and accuracy.

Recommendation: decide after step 0. If both do equally well, take B for its lower request count; otherwise take A.

3 · Unsure matches

Pockets marked Check could start ticked, so one tap adds everything, or unticked, so you confirm each.

Recommendation: unticked. Only Sure matches start ticked, as you asked to confirm the cards first.

4 · Your test photos

Step 0 needs 3 photos of real pages from your binder, taken with your iPhone.

Send them in this chat when you're ready; they're used for testing only and kept out of the repo unless you say otherwise.
